#### H2: How the fit works

BestBikeFit4U is designed to make bike fitting easier to start and easier to act on. Riders enter their measurements, add a bit of riding context, and then review fit guidance that is built to feel practical rather than abstract. The goal is to reduce guesswork and give the rider a clearer starting point for setup decisions.

#### H2: Why riders start here

The homepage should make it clear that BestBikeFit4U is built around those real rider moments.

#### H2: When an in-person fit is the better option

Others may need medical support if symptoms are severe, complex, or persistent away from the bike. This section should increase trust by being honest about scope, not by trying to claim every use case.

#### H2: Start with your measurements

The easiest next step is to begin with the bike and body information you already have. A rider does not need a full workshop, advanced technical knowledge, or a stack of new parts to get started. The homepage should end by making the first action feel simple, concrete, and low-friction.

#### H2: What lower-back pain usually feels like

Lower-back discomfort often builds over time rather than appearing instantly. Riders may notice it after 30 to 90 minutes, especially when they stay low for long periods or ride harder than usual. Many also feel partial relief when they sit up or change hand positions, which is a strong clue that support and load tolerance are involved.

#### H2: The fit drivers that usually matter

Reach, bar drop, saddle support, and pelvic stability interact more than most riders expect. A cockpit that is too long or too low can force the lower back to brace continuously, while an unstable saddle setup can make the torso work harder than it should. The page should encourage a whole-contact-system review, not isolated guesses.

#### H2: How to reduce strain without overcorrecting

This section should encourage conservative changes and structured testing. A slightly shorter reach or a modest reduction in drop can make the position more sustainable without flattening the fit entirely. Riders should be guided to separate saddle changes from cockpit changes so they can tell what actually improved the symptom pattern.

#### H2: When to get extra support

If the pain is persistent, radiating, or present away from the bike, the rider should stop iterating alone. A structured fit review can still help on the mechanical side, but ongoing back pain should not be treated as a fit-only problem when symptoms suggest something broader.

### H2 Structure With Publish-Ready Copy

#### H2: How hand numbness usually shows up

Hand numbness often appears in the fingers, the palm, or both hands together. It may show up sooner on rough roads, longer rides, or when the rider is locked into one position for too long. The most useful clue is whether the symptom improves when the rider sits more upright or changes how much weight is being carried through the bars.

#### H2: The fit factors most likely to drive it

Reach, bar drop, hood angle, and saddle support all affect how much load ends up in the hands. If the front end is too long or too low, the hands often become the easiest place for the body to offload pressure. The page should encourage the rider to review saddle support and cockpit position together.

#### H2: What to change before blaming gloves or tape

Gloves and bar tape can improve comfort at the margins, but they usually do not solve the underlying distribution problem. Start with reach and front-end position, then confirm that the saddle is stable enough to support the rider properly. This is where the fit-first message should be strongest.

#### H2: When numbness needs more than a fit tweak

If numbness is persistent, asymmetric in a way that does not fit the riding position, or present away from the bike, the rider should stop self-adjusting indefinitely. A fitter can help review the setup, but symptoms that suggest something more than bar pressure deserve clinical attention as well.
